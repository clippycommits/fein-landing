The problem

Your edge is proprietary deal flow.
Nobody at the firm can see it.

  • Coverage is a guess. You have thousands of relationships with bankers, brokers, accountants, lawyers, and operators. The CRM says one thing. The inbox says another. Nobody knows which is true.
  • Relationships go quiet without anyone noticing. An intermediary who sent you three deals goes eleven months without a call. You find out when the next one goes to somebody else.
  • Ownership leaves when people do. The partner who held the relationship moves firms. What they knew about it was never written down anywhere a colleague can read.
  • Deal history is unsearchable. A company you passed on in 2023 comes back at a lower multiple. The memo, the reason, and the banker who brought it are in three different systems and one person's memory.

Questions

What deal teams ask first.

We already have DealCloud. Why would we add this?

A CRM is a database your team fills in by hand, and it is only as current as the last person who remembered to update it. fein is not a CRM and it replaces nothing. It reads your CRM and everything around it, resolves the whole picture into one graph, and keeps that graph current without anyone touching a field. It answers questions your CRM cannot: the strength of a relationship measured from real behaviour, the intermediaries drifting quiet, and the reason you passed on a deal three years ago with the document that says so.

Where does our data go?

Nowhere. fein runs on your infrastructure. It sends nothing to us, it has no telemetry, and there is no control plane of ours in the path. Every line of the code that touches your data is public and you can read it before you run anything.

What do we actually have to do?

Two short calls. On the first we connect your email, calendar, Drive, LinkedIn, and CRM. We do everything in between. On the second, fourteen days later, you check the answers against what you already know, and then it goes live.

How far back does it read?

As far back as your mail retention goes. Most firms hand us years on the first pass, which is the point: on day fourteen it answers like a colleague who has been at the firm the whole time.

Can we just run it ourselves?

Yes. It is MIT licensed and the repository is public. The same code we deploy for clients is the code you would clone. The paid offer is the build and the upkeep, so your engineers can build on top of the graph instead of maintaining connectors.

What happens if we cancel?

Nothing stops. It runs on your servers, so the graph, the connectors, and every answer stay where they are and keep working.
