What platform teams ask first.

We already track the portfolio in Notion and Airtable. Why do we need this?

A tracker holds what somebody typed in after the meeting. It cannot tell you that a promise made at the June board is still open, or that the candidate a founder needs emailed a partner last week. fein reads those sources directly and keeps the record current with nobody maintaining it. The tracker can stay; fein reads it too.

Where does our data go?

Nowhere. fein runs on your infrastructure and sends nothing to us: no telemetry, no control plane of ours in the path. Every line of code that touches your data is public, so you can read it before you run anything.

What access does your engineer have?

The engineer works inside your environment, on access your IT grants and can revoke at any time. Our people are bound by confidentiality, and access is limited to those who need it to support the service. It ends when the build ends, unless you ask us to keep the connectors supported.

Two limits we will not overstate. fein does not today keep a tamper-proof audit trail of administrative access; your own logging is the record that counts. And the graph sits on your machine unencrypted above the volume, so whoever you give host access to can read it. That is true of your other systems too, and it is why the machine is yours rather than ours.

Can board material stay restricted to the partner who holds the seat?

Yes. A board pack can stay in the private layer of the partner who holds the seat while the promise ledger and the support record stay shared. fein enforces that scoping inside every query. The detail is on the security page.

How far back does it read?

As far back as your mail retention allows. Most firms give us years of history on the first pass, which usually covers each company from the first meeting on.

What happens if we cancel?

Nothing stops. fein runs on your servers, so the graph, the connectors, and every answer stay where they are and keep working.
