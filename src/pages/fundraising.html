What IR teams ask first.

We track LPs in a spreadsheet and it mostly works. Why change?

It works until the raise opens. A spreadsheet holds what somebody typed, on the day they typed it. It does not know that an anchor went quiet 8 months ago, or that a promise from the AGM is still open. fein keeps all of that current with nobody maintaining it. The spreadsheet can stay; fein reads it too.

Where does our data go?

Nowhere. fein runs on your infrastructure and sends nothing to us. There is no telemetry, and no control plane of ours in the path.

What access does your engineer have?

The engineer works inside your environment, on access your IT grants and can revoke at any time. Our people are bound by confidentiality, and access is limited to those who need it to support the service. It ends when the build ends, unless you ask us to keep the connectors supported.

To be plain about the limits: fein does not today keep a tamper-proof audit trail of administrative access, so your own logging is the record that counts. The graph also sits unencrypted above the volume, and anyone with host access can read it. That is true of your other systems too, and it is why the machine is yours.

Can the IR desk use it without opening the deal side to everyone?

Yes. Access is per person, and fein enforces that scoping inside every query. A partner’s LP correspondence can stay theirs while the coverage map stays shared. The detail is on the security page.

How far back does it read?

As far back as your mail retention allows. Most firms give us years of history on the first pass, which usually covers the whole of the last raise. On day 14 fein answers like a colleague who sat through every LP meeting your firm has had.

What happens if we cancel?

Nothing stops. fein runs on your servers, so the graph, the connectors, and every answer stay where they are and keep working.
